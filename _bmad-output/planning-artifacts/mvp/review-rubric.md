# PRD Quality Review — AI Orchestrator MVP — dev loop

Reviewed: `prd.md` and `addendum.md` (2026-08-26). Calibration: internal tool, single operator, one-week build, chain-top (feeds `bmad-architecture` and `bmad-create-epics-and-stories`). Because the PRD's spine is the agent contract with `bmad-build-auto`, the review checked the PRD's claims about that skill against the installed skill (`~/.claude/skills/bmad-build-auto/`). Several findings below come from that comparison rather than from the PRD alone.

## Overall verdict

This is a real build contract, not a template fill: the Vision names the bet ("a dispatcher and a readout, not an autonomous engineer"), every FR carries testable consequences with exact strings, Non-goals and Out-of-scope do actual work, and the counter-metrics are the most thoughtful part of the document. What is at risk is the core loop itself: the PRD's build → verify → commit model does not match what `bmad-build-auto` does (it commits before it exits, halts on a dirty tree, and treats a `done` file as a review pass), and the three failure paths an unattended tool lives on — interruption, hang, and skip — are underspecified. Fix the three high findings in §4.4–4.6 and the document is ready for architecture and stories; leave them and the day-6 restart test and the "trust the green" promise will both surprise the builder.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions. §1 draws the line ("The MVP is a **dispatcher and a readout**, not an autonomous engineer"); §5 Non-goals are phrased as refusals ("It never merges, never pushes to a default branch, never force-pushes"); §6.2 names what was given up and the workaround ("one Run, one Repository … the workaround is two Runs"). Addendum §C records seven rejected alternatives with reasons, which is where a reader pushing back on the board design or the YAML editor finds the objection already answered.

The Open questions (§8) are genuinely open: items 1–4 and 6 are day-1 spikes with a named consequence each ("Decides whether `codex` is a full peer on day 4"), and item 5 is a real fork with a default chosen (FR-13 assumption) and a flip path written in addendum §D ("If this proves confusing … flip to hold"). The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts in §6.2 sit at real tensions (multi-repository epics; `unit_test_command` as a Step).

### Findings
- **low** OQ 1 covers Codex but not `local` (§8 item 1) — `bmad-build-auto` `workflow.md` makes subagents mandatory ("If you cannot, HALT with status `blocked` and blocking condition `no subagents`"). The question "does this harness satisfy the subagent requirement" applies to OpenCode as much as to Codex, and OpenCode is the harness most likely to slip (addendum §F). *Fix:* widen OQ 1 to "Codex and OpenCode", or add a seventh question.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here is furniture. One named protagonist, three UJs that each earn an FR (UJ-1 → Clone check and form retention in FR-4; UJ-2 → pre-flight and queue in FR-8/FR-13; UJ-3 → Needs you, Answer, Checkpoint in FR-18/19). The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD: it names `bmad-build-auto`, `stories.yaml`'s checkpoint fields, and DBOS as the reason "it exists now". NFRs carry product-specific bounds — NFR-2 "`skipped` and `unvalidated` are words, never green … Tested by a Template with a Step lacking `gate`", NFR-4's egress allowlist by host, NFR-7 "first run from clone in under 30 minutes on the Mac Mini". SM-3 honestly declares "no target, this is the comparison the three-harness design exists to produce".

### Findings
- **low** UI micro-spec inside FRs (FR-21 "Copy confirms in place (`COPIED`, ~1.2 s); the string never soft-wraps"; FR-24 "updates apply as a state swap without animation") — not theater, but a second source of truth for detail that `EXPERIENCE-v1.md` owns. When the experience spec changes, these lines drift. *Fix:* keep the testable behaviour ("copy confirms in place") and move the numbers to the experience spec, or mark them as quoted from it.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated and the features follow it: "a story-sized unit of work runs to a verified commit without anyone watching" (§1) → FR-9..12 (loop, durability), FR-16 (contract), FR-18..20 (the only four stops). The second leg — "compare harnesses and models on the same work" (§2.1) — is served by FR-14, FR-26..28 and SM-3. Success metrics validate the thesis rather than counting activity: SM-1 unattended completion, SM-2 cold-reload (directly the §2.1 contextual job), SM-4 recovery. The counter-metrics are excellent — SM-C1 names exactly how SM-1 would "look better while meaning less", SM-C2 names the failure mode where agents assume instead of block. Scope kind is problem-solving with a platform edge (three harnesses), and §6 is consistent with that.

### Findings
- **medium** No priority tier inside §4 / §6.1; the slip order lives only in addendum §F (§4.3, §6.1, addendum §F) — §6.1 lists "editable versioned YAML and rendered diagram" as in scope while addendum §F says "Slips first: OpenCode adapter, Templates editing, Runs pagination, cancel rollback". FR-5..7 are three FRs of platform (validator, immutable versions, diagram) for an MVP that "ships one Template" authored by the same person; the PRD does not say why they are week-one rather than week-two. `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` will read all 28 FRs as equal. *Fix:* add a must/should line to §6.1 or a "slips first" list mirroring addendum §F, and state whether FR-7 (diagram) and in-browser editing are required for the day-7 dogfood.
- **medium** SM-2's measurement is undefined (§7 SM-2; FR-23) — "measured as active time on the Run detail" and FR-23's "active Operator time in this Run" never say what active means (tab focused? time between open and submit? excluding idle > n minutes?). SM-2 is a primary metric and FR-23 has to compute it; without a definition the number is whatever the implementer picks. *Fix:* one sentence: e.g. "sum of intervals the Run detail tab is focused, capped per interval at 5 minutes idle, from open to submit".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The bar is high for most of the document: FR-1 gives lockout numbers, FR-8 lists every pre-flight check, FR-10 gives the commit message template and the exact retry count, FR-16 enumerates every routing outcome with its reason string, FR-22 names the empty-column rendering ("`—` and count 0"). NFRs have bounds, not adjectives. An engineer can write acceptance tests from FR-1..8, FR-13, FR-17..19, FR-22..28 as written.

The gap is concentrated where the PRD meets the skill it dispatches. §4.5's description says "The Harness is irrelevant to the outcome contract: the Story file is the result" — correct — but the PRD's model of what the skill does around that file is wrong in three places, and each one lands on a primary feature (FR-10 loop, FR-12 durability, FR-20 skip). These are verified against the installed `bmad-build-auto`, not inferred.

### Findings
- **high** The loop's commit ordering contradicts the skill's contract (§1 Vision, §4.4 description, FR-10, FR-20) — the PRD says "verifies the result with the repository's own quality gate, commits" and defines the loop as "{build → verify → commit}" with a commit Step that "adds the Spec folder and all changes … message `story {id}: {title}`". `bmad-build-auto` `step-04-review.md` finalization does its own commit before halting `done` ("Commit any reviewed-diff files that remain uncommitted … Do not push") and only halts `done` when "the version-controlled working copy is clean". So: the orchestrator's verify Step runs on already-committed code; the commit Step will record `no changes` on almost every Story; the commit message is the agent's, not `story {id}: {title}`; and FR-20 Skip after "verify failed 2/2" leaves the failing Story's commits on the branch that FR-11 pushes — the card then reads `n/n stories · pushed · PR` with broken commits inside. This is the "trust the green" promise (§2.1) failing silently. Also, FR-10's "one re-dispatch of the Story step with the failing output as context" re-dispatches on a file whose status is `done`; `step-01` routes `done` to "a fresh review pass … not a resumption", which may or may not act on the failing output. *Fix:* decide and state (a) that the agent commits and the orchestrator's commit Step is a sweep for leftovers (rename the loop `build → verify → sweep`), (b) what the orchestrator does to the branch when verify fails twice and the Operator skips — revert the Story's commits (`git revert`/reset to the Step's baseline revision) or leave them and print `skipped, commits retained` on the card and in the PR link, and (c) what status the Story file is reset to before the verify-failure re-dispatch (`in-progress` routes to implementation; `done` routes to review).
- **high** Interrupted Story step re-attempt will halt `blocked` on a dirty tree, not resume (FR-12, SM-4) — FR-12 promises "An interrupted Story step re-runs as a new attempt with a new Session id; the agent resumes from the Story file's own status." `bmad-build-auto` `step-01` §3 "require a clean working tree … HALT on a dirty tree". A process killed mid-implementation leaves uncommitted edits in the Worktree; the re-attempt lands in `needs-you` with `blocked`, and the day-6 test (SM-4 "Target 100%") counts that as a failure. The same applies to any Harness crash mid-edit. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-12 (or a pre-dispatch rule in §4.5): before every Story step attempt the orchestrator normalizes the Worktree — either commits WIP as `wip: story {id} attempt n` or resets to the last Step's recorded revision — and records which. Add "attempt after kill mid-edit" to the day-6 test.
- **high** No wall-clock limit on any Step (FR-10, FR-14, §4.6) — §4.6 says "the Run stops for the Operator in exactly four cases"; a hung Harness process (or a `quality_gate_command` that never exits) is a fifth case the PRD does not have. The Run stays `running`, the card stays green, the budget check in FR-17 never fires because it runs "After every Story step". For a tool whose thesis is "walks away", that is the one failure the Operator cannot see. *Fix:* a per-Step maximum (e.g. Story step 90 min, verify 30 min, both Template-declarable) after which the Step is `error` and the Run `needs-you` with reason `timeout {step} after {n} min`; list it as the fifth stop in §4.6.
- **medium** `## Human Answers` is a PRD convention, not a skill contract (§3 Answer, FR-18, addendum §D) — the glossary defines Answer as text "appended to a Story file under `## Human Answers`, which resets the Story's status and re-dispatches it". The installed skill has no notion of that section (no occurrence of "answer" anywhere in it); the addendum relies on a prompt sentence ("treat it as resolved intent") and on `draft` routing back through planning. That will probably work, but the PRD states it as mechanism, not assumption, and it is the whole UJ-3 path. *Fix:* tag it `[ASSUMPTION: the agent honours an appended Human Answers section when re-planning from draft]`, index it, and add it to the day-1 verification queue (§8 / addendum §G) — it is an hour to test.
- **medium** Worktree lifecycle after a terminal Run is unspecified (FR-9, FR-20, NFR-7) — only Cancel "removes the Worktree". A Run that ends `failed` (baseline non-zero, FR-9) or `done` leaves `/work/{project}/{repo}/wt/{working branch}` in place; a second Run on the same Working branch (the obvious next move after fixing a baseline failure) hits an existing worktree path, and FR-9 does not say whether it reuses, recreates, or refuses. Disk on the Mac Mini grows per branch forever. *Fix:* one consequence on FR-9: "an existing Worktree for the branch is reused after `git status --porcelain` is empty, else the Run refuses with the reason"; and one on FR-11 or §Constraints: Worktrees of `done` Runs are removed after push (branch stays on origin).
- **low** Step status for two FR-16 outcomes is unstated (FR-16) — "Any other status → Run `needs-you` with reason `unexpected status`" does not say what the Step status is (`error`? `blocked`?), and nothing says what happens when the Harness exits non-zero but the file reads `done`. FR-20 Retry acts on "the failed Step", so the Step status matters. *Fix:* state `error` for unexpected status; state that the file wins over the exit code and the exit code is recorded.
- **low** FR-3 `package manager` is a field with no consumer and no value set (FR-3) — stored, never referenced by any Step or pre-flight check, values not enumerated. Unlike `unit_test_command`, it is not called out in §6.2 as stored-unused. *Fix:* enumerate values and say what reads it, or drop it, or add it to the §6.2 stored-unused line.

## Scope honesty — strong

§5 Non-goals and §6.2 do the work: seven refusals, eight deferrals each with a destination ("long-term PRD M2", "Deferred until one container is saturated"), and the manual-estimate field kept "because it cannot be retrofitted" — that is the right kind of scope reasoning. Seven inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags on genuine inferences (lockout numbers, daily re-check, `gh` availability, lock release, polling, cancelled column, span parenting). Open-items density is 6 OQ + 9 indexed assumptions + 2 `[NOTE FOR PM]` — high in absolute terms, but five of the six OQs are sub-hour day-1 spikes (addendum §G) and the assumptions are all parameter-level. For a one-week internal build that is fine, not a blocker.

### Findings
- **medium** The MVP's definition of done does not say which Harnesses are required (§6.1, §7 SM-3, addendum §C, §F) — §6.1 puts "three Harnesses (`claude`, `codex`, `local`)" in scope; addendum §F says the OpenCode adapter "slips first"; addendum §C keeps "Codex or Claude Code pointed at LiteLLM for local models" as the fallback. The PRD body never states the minimum: is day-7 dogfood with `claude` only an MVP? With `claude` + `codex`? SM-3 (the reason "the three-harness design exists") has no floor either. *Fix:* one line in §6.1: "`claude` is required; `codex` is required for SM-3; `local` ships if OQ 2 and OQ 4 pass by day 4, else the addendum §C fallback is used" — or whatever the real answer is.

## Downstream usability — strong

This matters more than usual here (chain-top) and the PRD is built for it: a 20-term glossary with capitalized terms used consistently across FRs, UJs and SMs; FR-1..28, UJ-1..3, SM-1..5/C1..3, NFR-1..7 all contiguous and unique; every cross-reference resolves (FR-2→FR-15, FR-3→FR-4, FR-10→FR-14, FR-23→FR-18/21, every SM→FR, Constraints→FR-11/17/20); each §4 group states which UJ it realizes. Sections stand alone — FR-16 is readable without §4.4 because "Spec folder", "Story file", "Gate strength" carry their meaning from §3. Addendum §E's lineage table to the 08-24 PRD is exactly what an architect reconciling the two documents needs.

### Findings
- **medium** "gate record" is a data entity with no definition (FR-18, FR-19, FR-20, FR-23; not in §3) — four FRs write to it ("The Answer, actor, and time are written to the gate record"; "Both write a gate record entry"; "Skip records … the gate record") and FR-23 displays it, but nothing says what a record holds, whether it is per Run or per Step, or whether it is append-only. Story creation will invent it. *Fix:* add to §3: "**Gate record** — append-only list per Run of Operator decisions: time, Step, action (`answer | continue | skip | retry | cancel | budget-raise`), text."
- **low** Smaller undefined or ambiguous nouns — "the Question" (FR-16, FR-23, addendum §D; capitalized as a term, not in §3); "attempt" (FR-12, FR-20 — implies the Steps table carries attempts, FR-23's column list does not); "Stories strip" (FR-23 — only in `EXPERIENCE-v1.md`); "within one poll interval" (FR-18 — FR-24 defines the UI poll, but Answer → re-dispatch latency is engine-side; say which). *Fix:* glossary entries for Question and Attempt; a pointer for Stories strip; "within 5 s" or "within one engine poll" in FR-18.
- **low** The Privacy constraint has no FR carrying it (§Constraints "prompt and tool content are redacted in Langfuse by default; enabling content logging is a per-Project switch") — a per-Project switch is a field, an API, and a Gateway/Langfuse configuration; nothing in FR-2 or FR-27 mentions it, so it will not become a story. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-2 ("`log_content` flag, default off") and one to FR-27 ("generations carry no prompt/completion content unless the Project's `log_content` is on").

## Shape fit — strong

Internal tool, single operator, chain-top — the capability-spec shape is right and the PRD wears it: FR-dense, three UJs (not eight), one named protagonist, operational SMs (SM-4 recovery, SM-5 configuration-time failures) beside the user-facing ones.
