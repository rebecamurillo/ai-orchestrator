# PRD Quality Review — AI Orchestrator

*Calibration: internal tool, single operator, PoC. Market sizing, competitive positioning, persona sets, and GTM are correctly absent and are not scored. What is scored hard is done-ness (this doc feeds epics/stories directly) and internal consistency.*

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, unusually honest PRD — the thesis is stated, every out-of-scope item carries a reason, §12 names the central bet as untested and builds three cheap instruments for it, and §9's C-1..C-7 are real constraints discovered before code rather than boilerplate. What is at risk is internal consistency at three load-bearing seams: §1 promises "several pipelines running in parallel" while FR-47 mandates sequential epic iteration; FR-30 escalates on any non-empty `assumptions`, which as written likely converts the async cockpit into a synchronous one and contradicts the ≥60% no-intervention target; and §12 builds the "is this better than by hand?" instrument but never pre-registers the number that would answer it. Fix those three and this is build-ready; leave them and the first epic will surface them as rework.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and the cost of each is named. §3's out-of-scope list does the thing most PRDs skip: every exclusion carries the evidence that produced it ("`sdk-python` is a validator pinned to spec 0.8, last released April 2022"). §9's serialization note is a model trade-off statement — it names what is bought (signal correctness), what is paid (throughput), and the condition to revisit ("only when per-run environment isolation exists"). §12.2's "build nothing for this… record the trigger condition instead: *the first time someone other than the operator must approve a gate*" is a deferral with a tripwire, not a dodge. Someone pushing back on the oracle-independence design will find their objection already handled in the §5 "Deviation from stock BMad" note and addendum E.

Where it falls short is that the PRD has effectively **no open questions left**. §10 lists Q1–Q10 with nine "Resolved" and one "Partially resolved" whose remainder is itself resolved by FR-50/51/52. A draft-status PoC PRD with zero live unknowns is not a sign of completeness; it is a sign the register stopped being maintained. Real unknowns exist in the body and are simply not registered — what the pipeline does when an acceptance criterion cannot be expressed as a Playwright assertion, what an escalation that is neither "blocked story" nor "epic ready for QA" looks like in the inbox, what budget numbers FR-45 enforces.

The other gap is a decision the PRD declines to make: §12 states the question that "decides whether the project was worth building," builds FR-50/51/52 to measure it, and then never says what result would count as a pass. FR-50 exists precisely so the outcome cannot contaminate the estimate — the same discipline is not applied one level up to the verdict.

### Findings
- **critical** No success threshold and no evaluation window for the central bet (§12.1, §2) — the PRD derives `estimated_manual_minutes ÷ actual_human_minutes` (FR-51) but never states what ratio means "keep building" vs "this was not worth it," nor after how many epics or weeks the judgment is made. The pre-registration discipline FR-50 imposes on the operator's estimate is not imposed on the project's own verdict, so the outcome will be interpreted after the fact. *Fix:* add to §2 a pre-registered decision rule and window — e.g. "after N merged epics or W weeks, a sustained ratio below X, or a sustained 0% rejection rate on any gate (FR-52), triggers a stop-or-redesign review." One paragraph, written now, before any number exists.
- **high** The manual baseline is a hard prerequisite buried in the addendum's verification queue — addendum F.7 says "Establish a manual baseline on two or three comparable features — **before** the pipeline exists, or the 'better than by hand' question can never be answered honestly." That is a scheduling constraint on the whole project, sitting in a downstream technical appendix as item 7 of 7. If it is skipped, FR-50/51 lose their comparator. *Fix:* promote it into §2 or §11 as a dated prerequisite with the same weight §9 gives C-1.
- **medium** §10 is titled "Open questions" but functions as a decision log — nine of ten rows are closed. A reader scanning for live risk gets noise. *Fix:* rename to "Resolved questions / decision log" and open a short live-questions table seeded with the unregistered unknowns named in this review (Playwright-inexpressible criteria, escalation taxonomy, budget values).
- **low** §2 supporting-metric targets (≥60% epics without intervention, ≥70% first-answer resume success) are asserted without basis or provenance. Harmless for a PoC, but they will be quoted later as if measured. *Fix:* label them "initial guess, revise after 5 epics."

## Substance over theater — strong

Almost nothing here is furniture. There are no personas — correct for a single-operator tool, and §5 names Rebeca with concrete context (two repositories, one of them dogfood) rather than inventing a cast. §1 explicitly lists **rejected framings** ("not an OS, not an autonomous SWE agent, not a general workflow platform"), which is the opposite of vision theater: it constrains rather than inflates. §12.3 declines to do competitive positioning and says why ("Immaterial for an internal tool serving one operator. **Required before any decision to productize**") — a refusal with a reopening condition beats a padded section.

The NFRs largely escape NFR theater: NFR-3 names a specific mechanism ("Permission-bypass modes refuse to start as root on Linux and macOS"), NFR-5 gives a number, NFR-8 names a specific failure ("must not become a second, unaudited way to mutate the working directory"). SEC-1..SEC-9 are tied to a stated threat model rather than a checklist.

Two soft spots. NFR-2 — "The execution model must not change shape when moving to multiple containers — same architecture, more instances" — is an architectural aspiration with no testable consequence; it is real intent, but as an NFR it cannot be checked. And FR-27's gate-strength taxonomy includes `adversarial` ("independent agent instructed to refute") which appears nowhere else: no FR requires it, no template in addendum D2 uses it, no step declares it. It reads as taxonomic completeness rather than a capability being built.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-27's `adversarial` gate strength is dead vocabulary (§6 F5) — it is defined and never used by any FR, workflow template (addendum D2), or journey step. Downstream will build an enum value nothing populates and a reviewer may assume adversarial validation is in scope. *Fix:* either cut it from FR-27 with a line in §3 out-of-scope ("adversarial validation deferred"), or add the FR that uses it.
- **low** NFR-2 is unverifiable as written (§7) — "must not change shape" has no test. Its real content is C-3's decision. *Fix:* restate as a checkable constraint, e.g. "no component may hold in-process state that a second instance would need; all run state lives in Postgres."

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is explicit and load-bearing: move the engineer from *writing* to *specifying and verifying*, with oracle independence as the invariant everything else protects. The design follows from it — G1b's signature exists so the Playwright spec cannot mirror the implementation (§5), FR-28b enforces the ordering, the stock-BMad deviation note explains why a shipped skill is being overruled, and §2's counter-metrics are built around the one failure the thesis is exposed to (a gate degrading into ceremony). Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity, and "**Non-goal:** maximizing PR throughput" is stated outright. This is a coherent arc, not a backlog with headings.

What breaks coherence is the parallelism claim. §1 says the product moves the engineer to specifying and verifying "**across several pipelines running in parallel**." §5 step 8 depends on it ("She switches to the other project. Nothing requires her attention"). FR-47 then says "**No concurrent runs against the same repository branch. The PoC iterates through epics sequentially**" — the second sentence is global, not per-worktree, and the parenthetical confirms the PoC does not exploit the narrower per-repository scope. As written, the PoC delivers exactly one running pipeline at a time, which is the opposite of the headline. It also degrades the primary metric: §2 measures "Velocity, spec → PR merged" as wall-clock "including parked time," so with global serialization that number absorbs queue time from unrelated epics and stops measuring the feature it is attributed to.

### Findings
- **critical** §1's "several pipelines running in parallel" contradicts FR-47's "The PoC iterates through epics sequentially" (§1, §5 step 8, §6 F9, §9) — the core product promise and the execution requirement disagree, and §5's "she switches to the other project" only works if at least two runs are live. The reconciliation (parallel across worktrees, serial within one) is available in FR-47's own parenthetical but never made. *Fix:* pick one and say it in §1. If the PoC is genuinely single-run, change §1 to "one pipeline at a time in the PoC, an architecture that parallelizes per worktree" and fix §5 step 8. If parallel-across-repositories is in, delete "iterates through epics sequentially" from FR-47 and scope serialization to the worktree only.
- **high** Global serialization contaminates the primary velocity metric (§2, FR-47) — wall-clock spec→merge "including parked time" cannot distinguish work time from queue-behind-another-epic time, so the metric the PoC is judged on is partly an artifact of the scheduler. *Fix:* record queued-but-not-started time as a separate span (it is a timestamp the orchestrator already has) and report velocity both gross and net of queueing.
- **medium** FR-19's rework cap (default 2) makes §2's "Rework cycles per story: ≥3 is a warning sign" unreachable — if the cap escalates at 2, no story ever records 3, so the warning never fires. Neither location defines what counts as one cycle. *Fix:* define a cycle once (a returned-for-fix pass through the story workflow), then align the numbers.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

This is the strongest FR set I would expect from a PRD at this stage in most respects — FR-13 ("survives process restart, container restart, and machine reboot without losing position or repeating completed steps"), FR-9, FR-12d, FR-21, FR-24 (names the exact required fields), FR-28, FR-40a and FR-42 are all directly testable, and several FRs pre-empt the ambiguity a story author would otherwise have to invent (FR-3a's "skipped — never failed, and never reported as passed" is exemplary). Adjective-driven requirements are rare; "enough context to reload cold" (FR-34) is immediately enumerated, so it survives.

The failures are concentrated in escalation and lifecycle, and they are the kind that only surface during implementation.

FR-30 is the most consequential. "**Escalate to a human when an agent returns non-empty `open_questions` OR non-empty `assumptions`**" — the reasoning behind it (§6 F5, addendum E) is right, but the requirement has no threshold, no severity classification, and no dismissal path. Coding agents emit assumptions on nearly every non-trivial task. As specified, the escalation rate approaches 100%, which contradicts the ≥60% no-intervention target in §2, contradicts §1's "walks away to other work," and turns the inbox into a stream. Nothing states whether an assumption the human waved through once is remembered on the next story or in the FR-19a review loop.

The escalation taxonomy is also incomplete. FR-33 enumerates inbox item types as "blocked story / epic ready for QA," but FR-19 escalates on rework-cap breach, FR-45 escalates on budget breach, FR-16 escalates task failures, and FR-28b reports unmatched acceptance criteria. Four escalation sources, two item types, no mapping.

The admission criterion has no enforcing requirement. §3 states it as the gate on all work ("the change must be *fast to verify*"), Q5 calls it "the admission test for work entering the pipeline," but no FR implements it. FR-28b reports mismatches *after* G1b, at translation time — and states only that they are reported, not what the run then does (fail the story? park at a gate? proceed `unvalidated` per FR-28?). The gatekeeping the whole design rests on is prose in §3 and nowhere in §6.

Lifecycle: FR-49 says "Tear down the environment on completion, cancellation, or timeout," while FR-19a's review loop re-runs a rejected epic "resuming the existing harness sessions." If G3 sits after completion (§5 gate table), teardown has already destroyed the worktree and environment the review loop needs; FR-19c anticipates one adjacent case (already-merged PR) but not this one. Relatedly, FR-40 opens PRs "when an epic's workflow completes with all gates satisfied," but addendum D2 places PR creation *before* G3 inside the `epic` template — so either FR-40's precondition is wrong or the addendum's template is.

Smaller ones: FR-16 requires distinguishing "infrastructure failure (retry) from task failure (escalate)" with no classification rule — this is unimplementable without one. FR-2 makes context artifacts "retrievable by agent tasks as prompt context" with no bound on volume or selection method, which collides with C-6's pinned context limits. FR-45 enforces budgets with no default values anywhere in the document, in a product whose #1 primary metric is cost. FR-5 validates that "declared commands exist" without saying how a shell command's existence is checked. NFR-5's "parked for 30 days resumes correctly" is bounded by "the session-retention constraint in §9," but C-4's mitigation (Postgres-backed session store, FR-22) removes that 30-day limit — so NFR-5 either understates the requirement or contradicts FR-14's "indefinitely — days or weeks."

### Findings
- **critical** FR-30 escalates on any non-empty `assumptions` with no threshold or dismissal path (§6 F5) — as written this fires on nearly every agent task, contradicting §2's "≥60% of epics reach human review without intervention" and §1's "walks away to other work." It also never says whether a dismissed assumption is remembered across FR-19a review loops. *Fix:* require the agent to classify each entry (`blocking` | `material` | `noted`) in the FR-24 schema, escalate on `blocking`/`material` only, and add an FR for persisting human dispositions so the same assumption does not re-escalate on resume.
- **high** The §3 admission criterion has no enforcing FR (§3, Q5, FR-28b) — "the change must be *fast to verify*" is the gate the entire oracle design rests on and is stated only in prose. FR-28b reports criteria with no corresponding Playwright spec but does not say what the run does about it. *Fix:* add an FR making translatability a hard precondition — at G1b, any acceptance criterion that cannot be expressed as a Playwright assertion blocks the gate and must be rewritten or the story rejected from the pipeline — and state FR-28b's failure action explicitly.
- **high** FR-49 teardown destroys what FR-19a's review loop needs (§6 F3, F9) — the environment is torn down "on completion," and G3 rejection happens after completion, yet the review loop resumes the epic's sessions and worktrees. FR-19c covers the merged-PR case only. *Fix:* teardown must be gated on G3 disposition, not epic completion; state retention (worktree, branch, session ids) explicitly through G3 and for a stated window after rejection.
- **high** FR-33's inbox item types do not cover all escalation sources (§6 F5, F6, F8) — "blocked story / epic ready for QA" omits rework-cap breach (FR-19), budget breach (FR-45), infrastructure/task failure (FR-16), and unmatched acceptance criteria (FR-28b). Downstream will build an inbox that cannot render half its inputs. *Fix:* enumerate the full escalation taxonomy once, with the required context payload per type, and reference it from FR-33.
- **medium** FR-40 and addendum D2 disagree on when the PR is opened relative to G3 — FR-40 says "when an epic's workflow completes with all gates satisfied"; D2's `epic` template runs "open one PR per repo → **G3**." *Fix:* state that PR creation precedes G3 and that G3 reviews the open PRs, then correct FR-40's precondition.
- **medium** FR-16 gives no rule for distinguishing infrastructure failure from task failure (§6 F3) — the retry/escalate branch is unimplementable as written. *Fix:* name the classifier (exit-code ranges, exception class, or a step-level `failure_class` declaration in the DSL).
- **medium** FR-45 enforces budgets with no values (§6 F8) — no per-run or per-project default appears anywhere, in a PRD whose first primary metric is token cost. *Fix:* set a starting per-run and per-project ceiling and say where it is configured.
- **medium** FR-2 places no bound on retrieved context (§6 F1) — "retrievable by agent tasks as prompt context" with no size cap or selection method, against C-6's pinned `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_CONTEXT_TOKENS`. *Fix:* state how artifacts are selected per task and what happens when they exceed the pinned window.
- **medium** NFR-5 (30 days) conflicts with FR-14 ("indefinitely — days or weeks") and with C-4's own mitigation (§6 F3, §7, §9) — the 30-day bound comes from local harness files that FR-22 replaces with a Postgres-backed store, which has no such limit. *Fix:* restate NFR-5 as unbounded-by-design and name the actual retention policy the orchestrator sets.
- **low** FR-5's "declared commands exist" is undefined (§6 F1) — resolving the first token on PATH, dry-running, and parsing are three different checks with different false-negative rates. *Fix:* name the check.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and reasoned, not inferred. §3's out-of-scope list gives each exclusion a cause, and several ("Multi-tenancy, RBAC, SSO. Single operator in the PoC") state the stakes assumption directly. §12.4 is the standout: it names that the admission criterion "narrows the tool by design," lists what it excludes ("much refactoring, most performance work, and anything whose value is judged rather than asserted"), and draws the conclusion most PRDs won't — "**a good result does not generalize**." §11's six `[ASSUMPTION]` entries flag genuine inferences, and one is escalated to "the central untested bet." Open-items density is low and appropriate for the stakes.

One real omission. §4 resolves the shaping-session mechanism as "a custom web interface fronting **Apache Guacamole**, installed in the infrastructure" — a whole subsystem with its own auth, scoping, and audit obligations (SEC-9). It appears nowhere in §3's in-scope list, has no FR in §6, and addendum B notes "Guacamole is infrastructure the PoC's single-container assumption does not cover — it sits alongside, not inside," which also puts it outside NFR-1. A component this size entering through a prose section in §4 is scope arriving without being scoped.

The PRD uses no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, but §12 does that work better than callouts would, and §12.2 attaches a reopening trigger. No finding.

### Findings
- **high** The Guacamole web interface is required by §4 but absent from §3's in-scope list, from §6's FRs, and from NFR-1's single-container boundary — its only requirements are SEC-9's three verbs ("authenticate it, scope it to the project's worktree, and audit it") and NFR-8. Story creation will have no source for it. *Fix:* add it to §3 in-scope, add FRs covering session creation ordering (orchestrator creates, human resumes), scoping to the project worktree, and audit-trace emission per NFR-8; note the deployment exception to NFR-1.
- **medium** The assumptions index does not round-trip (§11) — the six `[ASSUMPTION]` entries exist only in the index; none is tagged at the point of use. "GitHub issues carry enough signal to seed a shaping session without a separate intake step" is load-bearing for FR-38 and §5 step 1 and is invisible there. *Fix:* tag inline at first use and keep §11 as the index.
- **low** SEC-6 and NFR-4 state the controls that mitigate the declared top threat as preferences (§7, §8) — "**Prefer** credential injection at the proxy" and "**Prefer** an explicit tool allowlist" are the two mitigations aimed squarely at §8's threat model ("prompt injection reaching an agent that holds credentials"), and a preference is not a requirement an implementer can fail. Severity is low only because the PoC is single-operator and air-gapped by convention. *Fix:* make both MUST for automated runs, with the exception path named explicitly.

## Downstream usability — thin

This PRD is chain-top — it feeds architecture and `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` directly — so traceability carries real weight here, and it is the weakest dimension. Individual sections do read well in isolation, cross-references resolve (`forged-idea.md`, `.memlog.md`, and `addendum.md` all exist), and §5's single journey has a named protagonist carrying her context inline. Gate ids (G1a/G1b/G2/G3) are used consistently across §5, §6, §12, and addendum D2, which is exactly the kind of anchor downstream needs.

The problems are the missing glossary and the FR numbering. There is no glossary, and the domain vocabulary is dense and overloaded. "**Spec**" carries three distinct meanings: the analyst/PM artifact (§5 step 3, "Produces the spec"), the epics/stories artifact that "names the affected repositories" (§5 step 7, FR-12c), and Playwright test files (FR-28b, "translate… into Playwright specs"). "Session" means a harness session (FR-21), a shaping session (§4), and an interactive SSH session (SEC-9). "Instance," "template," "workspace," "worktree," "oracle," "harness," and "gate strength" all carry precise meanings that exist only by inference. A story author extracting FR-28b alone cannot tell which "spec" precedes which.

FR ids are unique but neither contiguous nor ordered: F8 (Observability) runs FR-43..FR-46 and then FR-50..FR-52, while F9 holds FR-47..FR-49 — so a reader following numbers walks out of one feature and back. Worse, FR-12's letter suffixes span two different features: FR-12 and FR-12a are workflow viewing/approval in F2, while FR-12b, FR-12c, FR-12d, FR-12e are workspace and branch lifecycle in F3. Anything that groups by prefix will collapse two unrelated requirement families.

### Findings
- **high** No glossary, with three genuinely overloaded terms (whole document) — "spec," "session," and "instance" each carry two or three meanings across §4, §5, FR-21, FR-28b, and FR-11. Downstream extraction of a single FR is ambiguous as a result. *Fix:* add a glossary defining at minimum: spec (shaping artifact) vs `epics.md` vs Playwright spec; harness session vs shaping session vs interactive session; template vs instance vs run; project workspace (Phase A) vs worktree (Phase B); oracle, gate strength, review loop, rework cycle. Then use the glossary terms verbatim in the FRs.
- **medium** FR-12b–FR-12e are filed under F3 but numbered as suffixes of FR-12, which lives in F2 (§6) — FR-12 is "Read-only workflow viewing"; FR-12c is "create a branch and git worktree." Prefix grouping merges unrelated families. *Fix:* renumber the Phase A/B lifecycle requirements into F3's own range.
- **low** FR ordering breaks across F8/F9 (§6) — F8 runs FR-43..46 then FR-50..52; F9 holds FR-47..49. Harmless to meaning, confusing to navigate and to diff. *Fix:* renumber or add a short FR index.

## Shape fit — strong

Correctly shaped. This is a capability spec for a single-operator internal tool, and it behaves like one: no persona set, one journey with a named protagonist used to sequence gates rather than to perform user empathy, operational metrics (cost per issue, spec→merge wall-clock, gate rejection rate) rather than user-facing ones, and no market or competitive section — with §12.3 stating explicitly why that absence is correct and when it stops being correct. §9's constraint section is the right kind of over-formalization for a product whose main risk is durability, and it is proportionate: seven constraints, each with a mitigation.

The only shape observation is a mild one. §5's journey is doing double duty as both the narrative and the de-facto lifecycle specification — steps 2, 7, and 11 carry sequencing detail (workspace before branches, branch only in touched repositories, one PR per repository) that is also in FR-12b/12c/FR-40. That duplication is currently consistent, but it is two sources for the same rule, and the FR-40/D2 disagreement above shows what happens when they drift. For this shape I would not add UJ ids or more journeys — they would be overhead.

### Findings
- **low** §5's journey duplicates lifecycle rules that also live as FRs (§5 steps 2/7/11 vs FR-12b, FR-12c, FR-40) — currently consistent, but it is a second source of truth and the FR-40 vs addendum D2 drift shows the failure mode. *Fix:* keep §5 narrative and point each step at its FR, so the FR remains the single normative statement.

## Mechanical notes

- **Glossary:** absent. See the Downstream usability finding — "spec," "session," "instance" are the three that will actually cause errors.
- **Term drift:** "epic instance" (§5 step 6) vs "workflow instance" (FR-11) vs "instances" (FR-11c, addendum D2) for the same concept. "Harness" vs "agent" vs "live agent session" (§3 in-scope) used interchangeably. "Gate" is used for both the four human gates and FR-27's "gate strength" on every step — two different objects sharing a noun.
- **ID continuity:** all FR ids are unique. Not contiguous: F8 holds FR-43..46 + FR-50..52 while F9 holds FR-47..49. FR-12's suffixes span F2 (FR-12, FR-12a) and F3 (FR-12b..12e). NFR-1..8, SEC-1..9, C-1..7, Q1..Q10 are all clean and contiguous.
- **Cross-references:** all resolve. `_bmad-output/forge/agent-workflow-os/forged-idea.md`, `.memlog.md`, and `addendum.md` exist. Internal §-references (§2, §3, §4, §9, §12) all point at real sections. FR-to-FR references (FR-3a→FR-28, FR-19b→FR-19, FR-31→FR-30, FR-7b→FR-9, FR-22→§9) all resolve.
- **Assumptions index roundtrip:** one-way. Six entries in §11, zero inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags in §1–§10. Every index entry is substantive; none is orphaned.
- **`[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts:** none used. §12 serves the function more effectively; no action needed.
- **UJ protagonists:** one journey, protagonist named (Rebeca), context carried inline (sole operator, two repositories, one dogfood). No floating journeys.
- **Required sections for stakes/type:** all present. Market sizing, competitive analysis, personas, pricing, and GTM are correctly absent for an internal single-operator PoC and should not be added.
